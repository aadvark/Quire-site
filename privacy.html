Privacy policy

Draft. Last updated [date]. Have this checked before the public launch.

The short version

Quire doesn't collect, store or send any personal data to its maker. There are no accounts, no analytics and no ads. Two optional features involve other companies, and only if you turn them on: searching the web for words you follow uses Bing News, and Translate uses Google's on-device translator, which sends Google anonymous usage statistics.

What stays on your phone

The sites and words you follow, the articles Quire downloads, your magazines, highlights and notes, covers you upload and what you've read are stored only on your device. Uninstalling Quire deletes them. Backups are files you save yourself, wherever you choose.

What Quire connects to

To show you articles, Quire connects directly to the websites you add, the same way a browser does. That includes YouTube, Reddit, Bluesky, Mastodon, Medium and podcast hosts when you follow channels, communities, accounts or podcasts there. Those sites can see an ordinary web request from your phone, including its IP address, and their own privacy policies apply. Podcast episodes stream from the podcast's own host when you press play, and videos open in the YouTube app or your browser.

When you add an Apple Podcasts link, Quire asks Apple's public podcast lookup for the show's feed address. To check for new versions of Quire, it asks GitHub for the latest release. Quire doesn't route anything through its own servers, because it has none.

Following words on the web (optional)

If you turn on “Also search the web” for a word you follow, Quire asks Bing News for recent articles that mention it, about once an hour. Bing (Microsoft) receives that word and an ordinary web request from your phone, including its IP address; Microsoft's privacy statement applies. The articles themselves then load straight from the publishers' own sites. If you don't turn it on, nothing is sent to Bing.

Translate (optional)

Quire notices an article is in another language using Android's own on-device language detection; nothing is sent anywhere for that.

If you tap Translate and agree, Quire uses Google ML Kit's on-device translator. The first time for each language, it downloads a language pack from Google. Translation then happens on your phone: the article's text is not sent to Google or anyone else. ML Kit does send Google usage statistics for diagnostics: your phone's make, model and Android version, Quire's name and version, a per-installation identifier that isn't meant to identify you, performance figures, and which languages you translate between. Google describes this on its ML Kit data disclosure page. If you never use Translate, ML Kit never starts.

Read aloud, notifications and the widget

Read aloud uses the text-to-speech voice installed on your phone; that voice's own privacy terms apply. The morning edition notification and the home-screen widget are made on your phone from articles already on it.

Children

Quire isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone.
